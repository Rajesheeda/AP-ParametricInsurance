Parametric insurance that checks the field before it pays.

Rainfall and cyclone triggers say something happened. Sentinel-1 radar, filtered through each farmer's e-Panta crop booking, says crops were really hit. Where they agree, payment is fast; where they disagree, a geotagged photo or a field visit decides. Every rule is a published method, and the system is tested against the Department's own crop-cutting yields.

Product design

WBCIS term-sheet structure

  • Season split into FAO-56 crop stages, starting from the median e-Panta sowing date of that crop in that mandal.
  • Per stage: deficit rain, excess (max 3-day) rain, dry spell (days < 2.5 mm, IMD).
  • Each value → percentile in the mandal's own 1995–2024 CHIRPS record → standard score (as SPI). Stage stress = worst peril; season stress = water-demand-weighted sum.
  • Trigger = 1-in-5-year season stress; exit = 1-in-25. Payout rises linearly to 100% of sum insured.
  • Premium = pure burn cost (mean payout 1995–2024); the insurer adds loading.

    Known limits

    • Real CCE yields cover YSR Kadapa only (26 mandals incl. Chapad and Khajipet, 2019–2025); none for Palnadu, Manyam or Chittoor, and no PMFBY claim amounts. Weather triggers alone fall short of the ≥75% target.
    • No APSDMA flood maps were shared, so flood accuracy is shown by a positive control declared in advance, not an ≥80% score.
    • Parcel boundaries exist for YSR only (about 56% of villages); 8.5k APAIMS polygons share an ID and are excluded.
    • Sentinel-1 meets the 72-hour window in under half the events; the daily rainfall trigger and photo verification cover the gap.
    • Radar cannot tell disaster flooding from paddy puddling; the e-Panta planting-window gate sends those cases to verification.
    • CHIRPS lags about a month; the most recent days use ERA5, marked provisional.
    • Telugu alerts are machine-drafted; no real field photos from the pilot mandals have been collected yet.
