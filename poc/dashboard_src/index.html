Insurance pays on the index. Relief checks the field. Never mixed.

Lane 1, insurance: a pure parametric dual trigger. A rainfall index and a satellite crop index must both cross pre-agreed levels, and an index enters the contract only after it beats chance against the Department's own crop-cutting yields; the payout is a formula on each farmer's own e-Panta sowing date, with no claim and no field visit. Lane 2, relief: a 72-hour disaster assessment. Sentinel-1 radar maps flooded standing crops and quality-checked, parcel-locked photos feed SDRF enumeration. Where the lanes disagree, a basis-risk log recalibrates the index each season against the Department's own crop-cutting yields.

Decision engine

Two lanes, never mixed

Insurance is a contract on an index: it must pay the same answer whoever looks. Relief is an assessment of real damage: it must look at the field. Mixing them is how parametric schemes lose their speed and their objectivity.

Gurazala 2023: insurance pays

Mid-season deficit trigger fired and crops looked stressed from space (VCI 30). Both indices crossed their levels, so the formula pays: no claim, no visit.

Chapad 2023: insurance does not pay

Rainfall said drought, but satellite showed green crops (VCI 45): irrigated paddy buffered the deficit. A rainfall-only WBCIS cover would have paid here; the real CCE yield shows no loss. Relief enumeration stays open for any farmer who did lose, and the case enters the basis-risk log.

Lane 1 · term sheet

One product, two covers, each a dual trigger

Cover A · Season (WBCIS structure, improved)

  • Trigger 1, weather: season stress from FAO-56 stages: deficit, excess (max 3-day), dry spell; 1-in-5 trigger, 1-in-25 exit.
  • Trigger 2, satellite: vegetation condition VCI < 35 (Kogan) in the same mandal and season.
  • Calendar: stages start at the farmer's own e-Panta sowing week. Each sowing-week cohort gets its own 1-in-5 level, so early and late sowers have the same payout frequency.

Cover B · Catastrophe (extreme rain, cyclone)

  • Trigger 1, weather: 3-day rainfall in the event, linear from the mandal's own 1-in-5 to 1-in-25 level. Index product: ERA5, the only one of CHIRPS, ERA5, IMERG and their 2-of-3 consensus that passed the qualification gate on YSR CCE.
  • Trigger 2, satellite: Sentinel-1 flooded share of standing cropland above the mandal's dry-season false-alarm level.
  • Settlement: when the radar image arrives (6–12 days). Total of A + B capped at 100% of sum insured.

Index qualification gate: what is allowed into the contract

    Product design

    WBCIS term-sheet structure

    • Season split into FAO-56 crop stages, starting from the median e-Panta sowing date of that crop in that mandal.
    • Per stage: deficit rain, excess (max 3-day) rain, dry spell (days < 2.5 mm, IMD).
    • Each value → percentile in the mandal's own 1995–2024 CHIRPS record → standard score (as SPI). Stage stress = worst peril; season stress = water-demand-weighted sum.
    • Trigger = 1-in-5-year season stress; exit = 1-in-25. Payout rises linearly to 100% of sum insured.
    • Premium = pure burn cost (mean payout 1995–2024); the insurer adds loading.

      Stated up front

      Known limits

      • Real CCE yields cover YSR Kadapa only (26 mandals incl. Chapad and Khajipet, 2019–2025); none for Palnadu, Manyam or Chittoor, and no PMFBY claim amounts. Weather triggers alone fall short of the ≥75% target.
      • No APSDMA flood maps were shared, so flood accuracy is shown by a positive control declared in advance, not an ≥80% score.
      • Parcel boundaries exist for YSR only (about 56% of villages); 8.5k APAIMS polygons share an ID and are excluded.
      • Sentinel-1 meets the 72-hour window in under half the events: relief uses the rainfall consensus and photos inside 72 h; insurance settles when the radar image arrives.
      • The dual trigger removes false payouts but cannot create detection: on YSR CCE most real losses (2021 floods, 2022) had no weather or satellite signal. Those stay with PMFBY area-yield and SDRF relief; the basis-risk log tracks them.
      • Per-farmer payouts are built for the YSR and Palnadu pilot mandals (e-Panta survey numbers; parcel polygons only in YSR); sum insured must come from the notified scale of finance.
      • Radar cannot tell disaster flooding from paddy puddling; the e-Panta planting-window gate sends those cases to verification.
      • CHIRPS lags about a month; the most recent days use ERA5, marked provisional.
      • Telugu alerts are machine-drafted; no real field photos from the pilot mandals have been collected yet.
